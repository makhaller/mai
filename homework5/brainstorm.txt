Particle Astrophysics 
Source: https://icecube.wisc.edu/science/research/ 
Summary:
This article discusses from the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, which detects
neutrinos, extremeley small, high-energy particles that travel through space.
Particle astrophysics studies particles like neutrinos, cosmic rays, and gamma rays 
to understand powerful cosmic events such as supernovae, black mholes, and active
galaxies. Because neutrinos ineract very weakly with matter they can travel across 
the universe without being signifcantly absorbed or defelcted, preserving information 
about where and how they were produced. Scientists measure properties of these particles, 
such as their energy, arrival direction, and timing, using detectors like IceCube. By 
tracing the direction they came from and comparing it with observations of objects in 
the same region of the sky, researchers cna identify the likely sources and study the 
extreme enviornments that accelerated the particles to such high energies. This helps 
scientists understand the physical processes, such as strong gravity, magentic fields, and 
particle acceleration, that occur in some of the most energetic events in the universe. 

The effects of low/zero gravity on biology
Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10351380/ 
Summary: This article explains how gravity influences biological systems from the cellular 
level to whole organisms. On Earth, gravity constantly acts on fluids, cells, and tissues,
helping determine things like fluid distribution, how cells orient themselves, and how mehcnical 
forces shape biological structures. In microgravity, these forces change significantly: fluids shift 
thoughout the body, cells experience different mechanical stresses, and signaling pathways that
depend on mechanical forces behave differently. The article describes how htese changes can 
affect processes such as cell growth, gene expression, and tissue organization. Studying biology in 
microgravity therefore helps scientists understand how physical forces inlfuence biologucal systems
and how organisms adapt when this fundamental force is removed.
